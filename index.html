A price ceiling is a legal maximum price. It is usually introduced to protect consumers from prices considered too high.

↗

If the ceiling is below equilibrium, quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied. The gap is a shortage.

THE KEY IDEA: A price ceiling below equilibrium can make a product cheaper for those who obtain it, but it can also create excess demand and non-price competition such as queues or rationing.

03 / THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK

THE RULES
OF THE VILLAGE.

The Indian government has legal powers to regulate essential commodities and medicine prices in the public interest.

1955
PRIMARY LEGISLATION

Essential Commodities Act, 1955

The Act empowers the Central Government to regulate or prohibit the production, supply, distribution and trade of essential commodities when necessary in the public interest.

SECTION 3(1) AND 3(2)(C)

Section 3 allows the government to issue control orders. Section 3(2)(c) specifically provides for controlling the price at which an essential commodity may be bought or sold.

Explore India Code ↗
2013
MEDICINE PRICE REGULATION

Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013

DPCO 2013 regulates the prices of scheduled medicines in India. The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA) implements important price-control functions.

PARAGRAPH 4 AND PARAGRAPH 19

Paragraph 4 deals with the calculation and notification of ceiling prices for scheduled formulations. Paragraph 19 provides for price fixation in extraordinary circumstances in the public interest.

Visit NPPA ↗
2020
HISTORICAL EXAMPLE

Sanitiser and Mask Price Controls

During the COVID-19 emergency, the government introduced temporary price controls for certain masks and hand sanitisers to protect consumers from unusually high prices.

HISTORICAL PRICE LIMITS

The March 2020 order specified a maximum retail price of ₹100 for 200 ml hand sanitiser, subject to the order's conditions. The order was valid until 30 June 2020.
